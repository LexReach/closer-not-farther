# Decisions and fallbacks

Every judgment call made during the unattended build, in the order it was made.

## Setup
- **Branch.** The session's harness designated `claude/bootstrap-prompt-execution-ag3d5w` as the working branch; the bootstrap says to push to `main`, and Pages deploys from `main`. Both branches are pushed with identical history after each step.
- **Network.** The sandbox proxy blocks Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, ebible.org, gnosis.org, earlychristianwritings.com and similar sites (403 on CONNECT, also for WebFetch). npm and Google Fonts CSS were reachable. Data checks therefore relied on web search snippets, raw GitHub text files, and the model's own knowledge; every such case is flagged below and in the JSON (`verify`, `verify_note`, `method`).
- **Fonts.** Loaded from Google Fonts at runtime (Fraunces, Source Serif 4, IBM Plex Sans, Noto Serif), with system serif and sans fallbacks in the stacks. The headless screenshot browser could not always fetch the font files through the proxy, so some screenshots show fallback fonts.
- **Routing.** History-API routes under the Pages base path `/closer-not-farther/`. `scripts/postbuild.mjs` copies `index.html` into one folder per route so deep links return 200, and to `404.html` for unknown paths.
- **Skeptic copy.** All "What skeptics say" points live in `data/skeptics.json` so that the About page can list them all in one place and every counterpoint has a source.
- **Dark mode** follows `prefers-color-scheme` only (no manual switch), as the spec describes.

## Module 1: Telephone vs. Tree
- **Alignment.** Copies are stored as slots aligned to the source words (omissions leave an empty slot; absorbed notes attach to the slot they follow). Real critics must collate first; the simulator skips that so the vote is the point.
- **Stable randomness.** Every draw is a hash of (seed, model, copy, word, purpose). Raising the error rate adds errors to the same copies instead of reshuffling, and a higher loss setting destroys a superset of the copies lost at a lower one.
- **What counts as recovered.** A word is recovered when the majority reading equals the source word with no absorbed note. A tie counts as unresolved.
- **The autograph is always lost** in both models; all copies (every generation) can survive.
- **Regions.** First-generation copies get a random region; each child stays in its parent's region with probability 0.8, otherwise moves.
- **Tree drawing.** A radial tidy tree (d3.hierarchy + d3.tree), because the maximum setting has 5,460 copies. Tree nodes are dots colored by number of changes or by region; the per-copy mini heatmap appears in the hover tooltip and the selected-copy panel. Chain tiles show the heatmap directly.
- **Harmonization** swaps from a small synonym list; where a word has no listed synonym, the error falls back to a spelling slip.
- **"Try to make the tree fail" preset:** 8% error rate, 90% loss, 2 copies per copy, 3 generations. Across 30 seeds the tree then recovers about 61% on average; at defaults it recovers about 99.7% vs about 73% for the chain (`npm test`).
- **Run** advances the seed and replays the copy animation; slider changes recompute instantly without animation. Once Reconstruct has been pressed, results stay live as settings change.

## Module 5: Names as fingerprints
- **Percentages** are computed at runtime from `data/names.json` counts divided by the stated totals. Bauckham's printed headline shares (`headline_stats`) differ from the recomputed ones (top 2 male names in Palestine: 17.6% recomputed vs 15.6% printed; top 9: 46.7% vs 41.5%). Both are shown side by side rather than silently choosing one.
- **`verify: true` values** (Gospels + Acts totals 79 / 18; Jonathan's Gospels count) could not be checked against the printed Table 6 from the sandbox. They are unchanged, marked with an asterisk in the chart, and explained in `verify_note`.
- **"Common name"** for the Twelve panel means in the top 12 male names shown in panel A (rank ≤ 12). Ranks come from `twelve.entries`.
- **Apocryphal names (panel C).** Populated for all four texts (37 entries) inside the ~$10 cap. The source sites were unreachable from the sandbox, so the lists were compiled from knowledge of the standard public-domain translations (Lambdin, Isenberg, the Gnostic Society text of Mary, Walker's ANF Protevangelium), with web search snippets as a check. Mention counts are approximate and flagged `approx`. The build details are kept in `apocryphal.build_note`; the UI shows `method`.
- **How panel C reads.** Instead of forcing apocryphal names onto the Palestinian frequency chart (their casts are mostly borrowed from the canonical Gospels), the panel shows, per text, how many names are borrowed and how the added ones are tagged. That is the fair version of the test, and it shows the complicating case too: the Protevangelium's additions (Joachim, Anna, Reuben) are ordinary Jewish names.

## Module 2: Closer, Not Farther
- **Minuscules added.** 17 medieval minuscules were added (`added_by_build: true`) to fill the lower right of the plot. Wikipedia and the INTF Liste were blocked, so dates and "year known" come from web search snippets and standard references; 13 of them carry `verify: true` with a `year_known_basis`.
- **Corrections to the supplied data:** P1 `year_known` 1897 to 1898 (publication year, matching the convention used for other papyri); minuscule 1739 1879 to 1899 (von der Goltz's publication).
- **Gap to the autographs** = midpoint of the earliest known witness minus 100 AD (end of the conventional composition window, stored in `manuscripts.json` → `autographs`). This reproduces the 50-year NT gap in `comparison.json`.
- **"Manuscripts available"** is a step function over `count_over_time`: the most recent catalogue figure at or before the selected year, labelled approximate, together with how many of the plotted landmark witnesses are known.
- **Starting year** is 1516 (the first preset), so the dots fill in as the reader drags forward.
- **Comparison panel** sorts works by manuscript count and draws count and gap as two separate bar charts with the same row order, so count is never shown alone.

## Module 6: Undesigned coincidences
- **Verse quotations** come from the World English Bible. ebible.org and bible-api.com were blocked, so the text was taken from a plain-text WEB copy on GitHub (raw.githubusercontent.com/nehemiaharchives/bbl) and trimmed to 30 words or fewer.
- **Reference fix:** the Pilate item's "Mark 15" (a whole chapter, used to make a point about an absence) now quotes Mark 15:2, 5, with the original kept in `ref_context`.
- **Graph layout:** fixed, one column per Gospel (Acts sits with Luke), nodes ordered by chapter. Each item links its first reference (the question) to each later reference. Shared passages (John 6:4) become shared nodes, which shows the interlock.
- **Map inset** is schematic, with approximate coordinates stored in `coincidences.json` → `map_geo`. Bethsaida is placed at et-Tell and labelled as one of two proposed sites.
- The featured Bethsaida example opens fully expanded; other questions open at step 1 and reveal one step at a time.

## Module 4: The 110% puzzle
- **Witness data corrections** (checked against Metzger's commentary and apparatus knowledge; the apparatus sites were blocked): Mark 16:9-20, Bezae changed from true to null (its Greek leaf for the end of Mark is lost); John 7:53-8:11, Alexandrinus changed from false to null (lacuna John 6:50-8:52). Every passage now has a `verified` basis and `witness_notes` for split or corrected witnesses (marked † in the chart).
- **Two readings instead of include/exclude.** Romans 5:1 and John 1:18 are choices between two readings, not insertions, so they carry an explicit `readings` array and `printed` (the reading in NA28/SBLGNT), and the toggle switches between the two readings.
- **Bezae in John 5:3b-4** has 3b but not v. 4; it counts as including the disputed span, with a note.
- **Witness chart** places each witness in its century band (P66 at "2.9", late 2nd/about 200). The treemap shares come from `categories.breakdown`, and the 400,000 figure is shown as a headline number, as the skeptics note requires.

## Module 3: Read P66 yourself
- **Image fallback taken.** Wikimedia Commons was unreachable from the sandbox, so no photograph could be downloaded or its license confirmed. Following the spec's fallback, the page draws an SVG facsimile (uncial letters in brown ink on a papyrus-colored page with a fibre texture) captioned "Facsimile rendering; photograph pending license." To switch to the photograph, save the Commons file as `public/p66-page1.jpg` and set `image.available: true` and `image.file` in `data/p66.json`; the page then overlays the word boxes on the photo.
- **Line breaks are approximate.** Published line-by-line transcriptions (IGNTP, NTVMR) were unreachable. `p66.json` gives 14 plausible lines of 23–24 letters carrying the standard wording of John 1:1–7, with nomina sacra ΘΝ/ΘΣ/ΘΥ, and says so in `line_breaks_source`. The page says the papyrus row is a reading guide, not a transcription, and makes no claim about P66's specific readings.
- **Word boxes** are computed at runtime from the line boxes and letter offsets, so a word that wraps across two lines gets a box on each line.
- **Title line** ("ΕΥΑΓΓΕΛΙΟΝ ΚΑΤΑ ΙΩΑΝΝΗΝ") is drawn above the text because P66 preserves the book title.

## Finish
- **Accessibility.** Lighthouse 12 (accessibility category, headless Chromium, light mode) scores **100 on all 8 routes**, after fixing heading order (source lists and cards use `h2`), the tree SVG role, ARIA roles on the count-chart points, and the contrast of glosses inside highlighted words.
- **Checks.** Every route was screenshotted at 1280px and 400px, light and dark, with no console errors apart from font requests the sandbox proxy refused. `npm test` passes.
- **Deploy.** The workflow builds, tests and uploads the site, but the deploy fails until Pages is enabled: the workflow token gets "Resource not accessible by integration" when it tries to create the Pages site. Pushing a built `gh-pages` branch from the sandbox was refused by the session's permission policy as a production deploy, so it was not attempted again. The owner needs to flip one setting (Settings → Pages → Source: GitHub Actions) and re-run the workflow.
- **Spend** could not be measured from inside the session. Four data subagents ran on Sonnet; the rest ran in the main session.

# Upgrade pass ("solid" to "best")

## Environment
- **The network was still blocked for the sandbox** at the start of the upgrade: Wikidata, Wikipedia, Wikimedia Commons, gnosis.org, earlychristianwritings.com, jsDelivr and the IIIF hosts all returned 403 from the proxy, for both curl and Node `fetch`. npm, PyPI and GitHub were reachable.
- **Fetching through GitHub Actions.** GitHub-hosted runners have open internet, so data acquisition runs there. Each fetch job lives on its own branch (`library-data`, `sources-data`), triggers on push to that branch, and commits its outputs back to the same branch. The results were reviewed locally and copied into `main`. `main` itself is never written to by CI.
- **Pages** had been switched on by the owner (run 8, re-run, succeeded), so the site is live at https://lexreach.github.io/closer-not-farther/. The Pages API itself is blocked through the sandbox proxy ("Access to this GitHub API path is not permitted"), so it could not be queried directly; `has_pages: true` on the repository and the successful deploy runs confirm it.
- **Fonts are self-hosted** with fontsource (Fraunces, Source Serif 4, IBM Plex Sans, Noto Serif Greek + Greek Extended). This removes the Google Fonts request, and with it the blocked-font console errors and a render-blocking stylesheet.

## Section 1: data integrity
- **Skeptic panels rewritten** (`data/skeptics.json`). Every point now cites a bibliography entry (author, work, year, chapter or page where known) and carries a one-line "Defenders reply" with its own citation. The Ehrman/Wallace framing (Stewart ed., *The Reliability of the New Testament*, 2011) appears in the telephone, timeline and variants panels, on the About page and at the end of the home page. The About page lists the full bibliography.
- Where no published skeptic source for an objection could be named with confidence, the citation says so ("objection as stated and answered in McGrew 2017") rather than attributing the objection to someone who may not have made it. The 2022 *NTS* article is cited only for the claim the spec attributes to it (Bauckham's arithmetic questioned); the eyewitness-authorship objection is attributed to Ehrman, *Jesus Before the Gospels* (2016), which engages Bauckham directly.

## Section 2: the home page as the argument
- **Opening frame.** A canvas miniature of Module 1 driven by the real simulator (seed 4, 16-copy chain, 3×4 tree, 3.5% errors, 50% loss): the chain recovers 70%, the tree 100%. It loops every 12 s, pauses when off screen or in a hidden tab, and reduced-motion users see the final frame only. The headline sits above the canvas rather than on top of it, so it stays readable at every width.
- **Chapters** are defined once in `src/chapters.ts` and reused by the home page, present mode and the tour. Each mounts real module components (the timeline `scatter`, `drawNameChart`, `TwelveGrid`, `witnessChart` + `passageText`, the P66 `facsimile` + `tokenBoxes`, the coincidences `stepsList` + `mapInset`, and a new `WordStrip` for Module 1). Module code and D3 load only as a chapter approaches (IntersectionObserver, 600px margin); excerpts play at 33% visibility and pause when they leave.
- **Chapter 1** shows the new "compare a single word" strips rather than repeating the hero animation: it cycles through the words the chain gets wrong and shows both models' readings.
- **Chapter 7 ("Here they are")** is the Library's featured shelf; see section 5b.

## Section 3: present mode
- `/present` is a bare route: no rail, fixed full-viewport stage, always dark (its own token set, not an inverted light theme). There are 10 slides: a title slide, the seven chapters with names split into "Names" and "The Twelve", and a closing slide pointing to the skeptics. Each slide mounts the same live excerpt as the home page with `big: true`, which switches the charts to stage metrics (28–30px labels, 4px lines, 12–14px markers) and compacts layouts to fit 1920×1080 without scrolling.
- Keys: → / PageDown, ← / PageUp, Home / End, Space (play/pause the live chart), F (full screen), ? (overlay), Esc (close the overlay, then leave). The progress rail at the bottom is clickable. Deep links use the slide id in the hash (`/present#names`, `#twelve`, `#library`), and the hash updates as you move.
- On stage the coincidence walkthrough shows one step at a time, with earlier steps collapsed to their reference chips, so four steps fit on one screen.

## Section 4: guided tour and film
- **Tour** (`src/tour/tour.ts`, narration in `data/tour.json`): nine chapters, about 2 minutes 50 seconds. It navigates with the app's own router and moves real controls: sliders animate through `input` events, toggles are clicked, buttons are pressed (briefly outlined so viewers can see what moved), P66 words are hovered in sequence. It has pause/resume (button or Space), skip chapter, end (button or Esc), a progress rail, and optional Web Speech narration (off by default). A missing control never stops the tour; that step's action is skipped. `?tour=1` starts it on load, and `&film=1` switches to the recording layout.
- **Film** (`scripts/render-film.mjs`): Playwright records the tour at 1920×1080 with the caption card burned in, then ffmpeg (libx264, CRF 26 and stepping up until the file is under 20 MB) writes `public/film/closer-not-farther.mp4`, a poster frame and a 20-second teaser (opening frame + timeline sweep). ffmpeg came from the `imageio-ffmpeg` wheel on PyPI (a static build with libx264), because apt was not available. If H.264 encoding fails, the script falls back to WebM.
- The film loads only on the home page, and only when its section comes within 400px of the viewport (`preload="none"`; sources are attached by IntersectionObserver).

## Phase A: the Reader
- **Data through CI.** The three data jobs (`data-bible`, `data-evidence` and `data-ot`) were started at the beginning of the session on their own branches. Each was run by a Sonnet subagent that wrote a `data-*.yml` workflow and iterated on it; the output was reviewed here before being copied to `main`. GitHub-hosted sources (MorphGNT, SBLGNT, STEPBible, morphhb) could also be read from the sandbox; bereanbible.com and ebible.org only from CI.
- **The first BSB build was wrong.** It used the interlinear column of `bsb_tables.xlsx` in Greek order ("In [the] beginning was the Word and the Word was with - God…"). The rule now is that the BSB text is `bsb.txt` verbatim, with interlinear rows located inside it in BSB order. The build fails if the segments do not concatenate to the verse. 95.8% of NT English words are aligned to a Greek word; the rest (punctuation, spacing, supplied words) are not.
- **Verse boundaries.** STEPBible TAGNT and the SBLGNT divide some verses differently (3 John 14–15, 2 Corinthians 13:12–13, Romans 3:26, and others), which left 559 Greek words without an analysis. `scripts/bible/fill-boundaries.mjs` gives each the most frequent analysis of the same word form elsewhere in the NT. That filled 466. The remaining 93 are rare forms, and the meaning card says that no analysis is recorded. Lexicon keys are zero-padded (`G0746`), so the Strong's search compares numbers, not strings.
- **Versions whose verse numbering differs.** Rows are the maximum verse count across the columns shown. Where a version lacks a verse (for example Romans 16:24 in the BSB and SBLGNT), its cell is empty. The John 7:53–8:11 passage is in the BSB but not in MorphGNT, which predates SBLGNT 1.2, so it has English with no Greek.
- **Original language loads after the English** unless the Greek or Hebrew column is shown. It is only needed for the BSB long-press, and awaiting it cost the mobile Lighthouse score about 6 points.
- **One-time hint.** "Tap any verse to see the manuscripts that carry it" is shown once the evidence panel exists (Phase B), since in Phase A tapping a verse only selects it.
- **Meaning card.** A Greek word opens it on tap, and a BSB word opens it on long-press (450 ms), which also lights every English segment that renders the word. It gives the gloss, lemma, transliteration, dictionary gloss, parsing in plain English (from the Robinson codes), Strong's number and the lemma's NT count, with a "Find all" search.
- **ESV.** `EsvAdapter` calls only the owner's proxy (`data/config.json` → `esvProxyUrl`). It fetches whole chapters and keeps at most 500 verses in memory, evicting the least recently used. The Worker in `proxy/esv-worker.js` holds the key as a secret, forwards only `passage/text` and `passage/html`, allows CORS for the site's origin, and rate-limits per IP. Crossway's copyright line and an esv.org link are shown under any ESV column. The mocked tests are in `scripts/reader-check.ts`.
- **Navigation.** The rail and the phone tab bar are Read · Library · Why · About. The six modules, present mode and "The film" are listed under Why, and the old home page is `/why`. `/` is the Reader. All old routes still work. The tour's intro and present mode's Exit now point to `/why`. The film was not re-rendered; a note under it says it was recorded before the Reader was added.
- **Library viewer.** It opens at the first canvas labelled folio 1, else the first after a cover, flyleaf or guard sheet, else the third canvas. It has previous and next buttons and a page field, and the page is kept in the URL (`#ms=04&p=12`).
- **Checks.** All 66 books were opened at their first and last chapters, and 156 meaning cards on sampled NT words opened without errors. Lighthouse on `/read`: mobile 94–96 performance and 100 accessibility; desktop 100 and 100. Repeat visits with the service worker show verse text 55–75 ms after navigation, and a chapter switch takes about 50 ms. A smoke job in the deploy workflow now opens the live site after every deploy and fails the run if the Reader does not show John 1, Romans 8 and Genesis 1.

## Phase B: the evidence panel (shipped with the data available when the budget ran short)
- **Coverage is page-level for 446 manuscripts.** The INTF page index (fetched in CI on `data-evidence`) covers 136 papyri, 245 majuscules, 21 minuscules and 44 lectionaries: nearly every early witness but few later minuscules. The panel and the coverage map say so, and they say the full count of copies is higher. Catalogue-level guesses ("contains the Gospels") are not used for papyri, lectionaries or majuscules numbered 046 and above, because a fragment cannot be placed on a verse that way. In the current data there are no catalogue-level hits at all.
- **Raw coverage stays on the branch.** The 32 MB of raw coverage is reduced by `scripts/evidence/derive.mjs` to per-chapter ranges (1.6 MB), per-book summaries and the coverage timeline.
- **Transcriptions.** The CI job's first 6,649 pages turned out to be the TEI XML split into words, headers included, so Phase B shipped without them. A later session rewrote the parser (`scripts/evidence/tei.mjs` on `data-evidence`) against the real NTVMR markup: 15,059 pages of 285 manuscripts, with lines, columns, verse tags, lacunae, sacred names and corrector hands. The raw page TEI is kept, gzipped, so the parser can be re-run without fetching again.
- **Dates.** The coverage map counts a manuscript from the latest century its catalogue date allows (P66, 2nd–3rd century, counts from AD 300). By that measure 41 verses are attested by AD 200, 4,293 by AD 300 and 7,944 by AD 400. Two catalogue errors were fixed: minuscule 1 (12th century, not 5th) and minuscule 137 (11th century, not 2nd).
- **NA28 "Most relied on".** The job's list of papyri and majuscules was flattened into `witness-tiers.json`, with the further majuscules and the minuscules from the NA28 lists added. It is marked as compiled.
- **Apparatus.** Parsed from the SBLGNT repository's own apparatus files (`scripts/evidence/sblgnt-apparatus.mjs`): 4,468 verses and 6,934 variation units. Each has the SBLGNT reading first and the edition sigla (WH, Treg, NA28, RP, NIV) on each reading.
- **Performance.** The panel renders in 150–200 ms after a tap, and a deep link to a verse opens it.

## Sacred names and Phase D (minimum)
- **Nomina sacra.** The table in `tei.mjs` was extended from a scan of every flagged word, not from a handbook list, so it covers the scribes' actual forms (χρυ, υις, ουνιος, σριας, πνικος, εστρωμενον, πρας). Derived words are handled by stem rules, which only ever see words the transcribers flagged. `ιη` is left contracted: in this corpus it is both Ιησους (P18 Rev 1:5) and the numeral 18 (05 Luke 13:16). Numerals are left as written. Words flagged only for a final-ν stroke (ημω, δοξα) are expanded too, since the flag marks the same overline.
- **Lost is not different.** In the Witness view, an edition word whose only counterpart is text the editor restored is faded as lost, not underlined. P66's θν in John 1:1 is supplied, so the papyrus does not read differently there.
- **OT versification.** The Reader's columns share English numbering, so the Hebrew is re-indexed with the `map` the data job took from morphhb's own KJV notes. A Hebrew verse with no English number (a Psalm title) is prepended to the next verse rather than dropped.
- **OT default columns.** An OT book opens with the Hebrew beside the BSB until the reader chooses columns. Anyone who had already saved a choice other than BSB alone keeps it.
- **OT witnesses are range-matched.** The 31 compiled witnesses carry verse ranges, not page indexes. The panel says so, dashes the fragmentary scrolls, and warns that a scroll's range can include lost verses. Dates count from the latest year the date string allows (4QGenb, c. 125–100 BC, counts from the 1st century BC), as the NT side does.
- **OT pages are drawn from the Leningrad text.** No OT witness here is transcribed or embeddable. The Witness view therefore draws the verse from the WLC, labels it honestly (for a scroll: the Leningrad wording without vowel signs, not the scroll's own text), and links out to the photographs.
- **Meaning cards in dialogs.** The card is mounted inside an open dialog, because the top layer hid it behind the Witness view (the NT had the same bug).

## Live-site fixes (after Phase D)
- **Hint as a toast.** The one-time hint no longer sits in the flow above the chapter. Anything that appears or disappears above the verses after first paint can move a verse out from under a tap.
- **Hash changes belong to the page.** The router re-renders only when the path or query changes. A same-page `navigate()` sets `location.hash`, so every page's own `hashchange` handler runs.
- **Deep links open the panel collapsed.** Someone who follows a link came to read the verse first. The one-line bar still states the count and the oldest witness. Tapping a verse inside the Reader opens the panel in full, as before.
- **Hebrew per chapter.** Per-chapter files are generated at build time, not committed, so the repository does not hold the Hebrew twice. In dev, the Reader falls back to the whole book.
- **Photograph first on cards.** A photograph says "this is a real object" faster than a transcription. The transcribed lines remain the fallback and fade in rather than replacing a placeholder.
- **Dead Sea Scrolls from one cited table.** Every biblical row of Wikipedia's *List of the Dead Sea Scrolls*, which cites Fitzmyer 2008 and links each row to the Leon Levy library, replaces the earlier hand-compiled spans. A scroll now shows only for the verses its fragments preserve. Rows that cannot be parsed or dated are logged, not guessed, including two well-known scrolls (MurXII, 11QPs^a) the table does not describe. Script periods are turned into years with the usual ranges and counted from the latest year they allow (Hasmonean c. 150–30 BC; Herodian c. 30 BC–AD 70; "Hellenistic-Roman" before AD 68, when the caves were closed).
- **Alexandrinus** is matched by its contents minus the two lost stretches the Wikipedia article gives, citing Würthwein. The Psalms gap is converted from the Septuagint's numbering to English numbering approximately, and the note says so.
- **OT headline.** "5 of the 244 listed witnesses" is followed at once by "Thousands of medieval Hebrew copies are not listed", so a small number reads as a limit of the list, not of the attestation.
